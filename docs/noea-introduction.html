Noéa
Reading water as rhythm, memory and mood
Affective computing for water systems
Hong Kong, 1884–2025: one beat per year. Height is the year's peak daily rainfall; spacing is the time since the previous peak.
Data can show that a river is changing. It rarely makes anyone feel it.
Noéa works in the missing layer: turning measurement into meaning people can experience.

4
Two layers, kept deliberately apart
Diagnostic layer
Plain science

Hong Kong rainfall, 1884–2025: annual peaks, the gaps between them, return periods.

Rhine and alpine rivers, 8 stations: flow duration, recession, the seasonal cycle.

Measures what happened, and is never adjusted for artistic reasons.

Curatorial layer
Designed

Decides how the results should feel: how alarming, how heavy.

Currently kept separate from the measurements, so the feeling could be redesigned without changing any of the science.

Its output: valence and arousal.

5
Machine learning finds unusual years
Its one job so far, unsupervised: learn what a typical year looks like, then score how badly each year fits.
The highlighted bar is each station's highest-scoring year. The model was never told these events existed.
Method: PCA reconstruction error, fit separately per station on its own years. Events: Aug 2005 Alpine floods (22–23 Aug), May 1999 Swiss floods (Thun, 15 May), Christmas 1993 Rhine flood (Köln, 24 Dec).
6
The end result is how people respond
Noéa connects machine intelligence to natural intelligence: a model learns the rhythm of a river or rainfall record and flags when it breaks.
How people respond to that now has to be tested in human labs, with HCI methods. That is the research.
